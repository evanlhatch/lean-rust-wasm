## The synthesis

The rework's architecture is sound and its gates are real: everything
pinned is green, the byte-tie holds, the consumed core carries the
product surface (schema -> emit -> oracle -> replay). The recurring
defect class is **landing phase-1 framework without its phase-2
consumer** — 13 of 20 pieces carry production load, 10 are carried by
tests alone, 7 by nothing. Three process fixes, in order of leverage:

1. **The canon-marker rule**: citations to pending adoption keep
   their `(W<n>)` marker; unmarked citations must resolve to tree
   artifacts. F1's cslib rows violate this today.
2. **No framework order lands without its consumer order paired in
   the same or the next wave.** The status board carries the pairing
   explicitly (W7.3p1->p2, W7.1p1->p2, kit->W6.11/W7.14).
3. **The delete-or-graduate list is standing** (below); every
   present-only piece gets a named consumer order or a deletion order
   at the next wave boundary.

## Purpose judgment (draft)

The vision: domain modeling + schema-driven codegen to Rust/WASM,
laws checked at the tightest tier. The end-to-end chain (schema →
WIT/Rust/vortex emission → wasm-backend → oracle → steel-host replay)
runs green under `just gates`, and the law surface is real where
pinned (substrait round-trips, dbsp certs replayed in Rust, machine
agreement theorems, the fp-literal bug found-and-fixed by the
findings process). The drift class this review keeps hitting is
**substrate ahead of adoption**: framework pieces land gated and
green but with consumption deferred to later orders (F2/F3, and the
pending W7.2p2/W6.11/W5.1 queue). That is the planned sequencing, not
a violation — but the canon table's citations must not claim the
adopted end-state before it lands. Rule proposed: canon citations to
pending work keep their `(W<n>)` work-order marker; a citation
without a marker must resolve to a tree artifact.
